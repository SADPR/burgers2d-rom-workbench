This document is organized as copy-ready input for a LaTeX-to-Image service.
All text intended to appear on a slide is included inside the LaTeX block.
Visual suggestions are written separately and should not be included in the
formula image.

The sequence deliberately uses the harmonic trajectory as the running example.
It first motivates nonlinear trial manifolds geometrically, and then introduces
the common LSPG formulation used by the paper.

Use the following title pattern throughout the presentation:


The first nonlinear-PROM slide directly applies a linear manifold to the

The post-slide-41 section outline is:

1. **Nonlinear PROMs: Harmonic example**
   Linear, quadratic, closure-based, and POD-AE nonlinear manifolds on the
   same trajectory.
2. **Nonlinear PROMs: Projection framework**
   Generic decoder, tangent, time-discrete LSPG optimality, and Gauss--Newton.
3. **Nonlinear PROMs: Model families**

4. **2D Burgers: Problem and protocol**

5. **2D Burgers: Comparative results**
   Linear reference, intrusive and non-intrusive model galleries, global
   accuracy/runtime summaries, and coefficient-data enrichment.

## General corrections

- Keep slides 1--41 essentially unchanged.
- In the current linear LSPG matrix cartoon, the unknown is
  `\Delta\mathbf q`, not `\mathbf q`.
- Use the LSPG-only framing used by the paper throughout this presentation
  segment.
- Do not obtain the intrusive online coordinates by projecting the unknown HDM
  state. They are computed by the PROM/HPROM residual solve.
- The paper's training targets are generated by the linear
  `n_{\mathrm{tot}}`-dimensional HPROM. They are not direct projections of HDM
  snapshots.
- Use `\mathbf q` as the generic online coordinate. Use `\mathbf z` only for
  the POD-AE latent coordinate.

```latex
&\hspace{3.2em}
```

### Slide 47 -- Nonlinear PROMs: LSPG optimality condition

```latex
\begin{aligned}
&\text{\bfseries Time-discrete least-squares objective}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{3.1em}
f(\mathbf q)
=
\frac12
\left\|
\mathbf r\!\left(\mathcal D(\mathbf q)\right)
\right\|_2^2
\\[1.3em]
&\text{\bfseries Residual Jacobian and manifold tangent}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{2.5em}
\mathbf J
=
\frac{\partial\mathbf r}{\partial\mathbf u},
\qquad
\mathbf T
=
\frac{\partial\mathcal D}{\partial\mathbf q}
\\[1.3em]
&\text{\bfseries LSPG test basis}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{4.4em}
\mathbf W
=
\mathbf J\mathbf T
\\[1.3em]
&\text{\bfseries First-order optimality condition}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{2.5em}
\nabla_{\mathbf q}f
=
\mathbf T^T\mathbf J^T\mathbf r
=
\mathbf W^T\mathbf r
=
\mathbf0
\\[1.3em]
&\text{\bfseries Orthogonality interpretation}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{1.6em}
\text{\itshape The time-discrete residual is orthogonal to the LSPG test space.}
\end{aligned}
```

Visual suggestion: show the product `\mathbf J\mathbf T` as a matrix diagram,
following the style of slides 35--36.

### Slide 48 -- Nonlinear PROMs: LSPG Gauss--Newton solve

```latex
\begin{aligned}
&\text{\bfseries Residual linearization}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{3.1em}
\mathbf r(\mathbf q+\Delta\mathbf q)
\approx
\mathbf r(\mathbf q)
+
\mathbf W\Delta\mathbf q
\\[1.3em]
&\text{\bfseries Gauss--Newton increment}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{2.6em}
\left(\mathbf W^T\mathbf W\right)
\Delta\mathbf q
=
-\mathbf W^T\mathbf r
\\[1.3em]
&\text{\bfseries Reduced-coordinate update}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{3.6em}
\mathbf q^{k+1}
=
\mathbf q^k+\Delta\mathbf q
\\[1.3em]
&\text{\bfseries Decoder-dependent test basis}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{2.8em}
\mathbf W
=
\mathbf J\mathbf T
=
\frac{\partial\mathbf r}{\partial\mathbf u}
\frac{\partial\mathcal D}{\partial\mathbf q}
\\[1.3em]
&\text{\bfseries Common intrusive solver}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{1.5em}
\text{\itshape The Gauss--Newton solve is unchanged; only the decoder and}
\\[0.2em]
&\hspace{3.4em}
\text{\itshape its tangent differ between model families.}
\end{aligned}
```

`\Delta\mathbf q` as the unknown.

```latex
\\[1.1em]

t\in[0,25],
```

## Appendix A: LSPG low--high transfer diagnostic

**Optional hidden slide.** Keep this outside the conference flow. Use it only
if someone asks why the residual-sensitive metric may matter for the
retained/discarded coordinate split.

**Left formula image.**

```latex
\begin{aligned}
&\text{\bfseries Residual sample and Jacobian}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{1.2em}
\mathbf r_s(\mathbf u),
\qquad
\mathbf J_s
=
\dfrac{\partial\mathbf r_s}{\partial\mathbf u}
\\[1.1em]
&\text{\bfseries Residual weighting}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{1.2em}
\mathbf P_s\succeq\mathbf0,
\qquad
\left\|\mathbf v\right\|_{\mathbf P_s}^2
=
\mathbf v^T\mathbf P_s\mathbf v
\\[1.1em]
&\text{\bfseries Linearized residual change}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{1.2em}
\delta\mathbf r_s
\approx
\mathbf J_s\delta\mathbf u
\\[1.1em]
&\text{\bfseries Induced state-space sensitivity}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{1.2em}
\left\|\delta\mathbf r_s\right\|_{\mathbf P_s}^2
\approx
\delta\mathbf u^T
\underbrace{
\mathbf J_s^T\mathbf P_s\mathbf J_s
}_{\mathbf A_s}
\delta\mathbf u
\end{aligned}
```

**Right formula image.**

```latex
\begin{aligned}
&\text{\bfseries Retained--tail perturbation}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{1.0em}
\delta\mathbf u
=
\mathbf V\delta\mathbf q
+
\overline{\mathbf V}\delta\overline{\mathbf q}
\\[1.1em]
&\text{\bfseries Coupling blocks}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{1.0em}
\mathbf H_{LL}
=
\mathbf V^T\mathbf A_s\mathbf V,
\qquad
\mathbf H_{LH}
=
\mathbf V^T\mathbf A_s\overline{\mathbf V}
\\[1.1em]
&\text{\bfseries Local reduced-coordinate balance}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{1.0em}
\mathbf H_{LL}\delta\mathbf q
+
\mathbf H_{LH}\delta\overline{\mathbf q}
\approx
\mathbf0
\\[1.1em]
&\text{\bfseries Low--high transfer diagnostic}\\[0.6em]
&\hspace{1.4em}
\delta\mathbf q
\approx
-
\mathbf H_{LL}^{-1}
\mathbf H_{LH}
\delta\overline{\mathbf q}
\end{aligned}
```
