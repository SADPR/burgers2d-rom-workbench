\begin{abstract}
We compare learned closures and nonlinear manifolds for parametric
projection-based reduced-order models of a nonlinear time-dependent system.
All models use a common Euclidean proper-orthogonal-decomposition (POD)
coordinate system and are trained from full-residual linear
projection-based reduced-order model (PROM) trajectories.  The comparison
includes state-conditioned, parameter--time-conditioned, and hybrid
\textsc{PROM-ANN} closures; an intrusive \textsc{PROM-POD-AE} decoder
manifold; a non-intrusive \textsc{POD-NN-ROM} direct coefficient map; and a
non-intrusive latent \textsc{POD-DL-ROM}.

The numerical study uses a parametric two-dimensional inviscid Burgers
benchmark.  The intrusive models all minimize the same time-discrete residual,
but over different learned trial maps.  A shared parameter--time map gives a
controlled sweep from a non-intrusive POD--NN--ROM endpoint to the
full-coordinate linear PROM.  A complementary tail-perturbation diagnostic
quantifies how a prescribed Case--2 secondary-coordinate error transfers to
the residual-solved primary coordinates.  A nested coefficient-data study
with 9, $9+8$, and $9+18$ PROM trajectories separates in-domain improvement
from extrapolation behaviour.  We also state the empirical
cubature method (ECM) formulation required for a future HPROM extension; all
numerical results reported here are full-residual PROM results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Projection-based model order reduction (PMOR) reduces a high-dimensional model
while retaining its governing residual and its parameter dependence
\cite{antoulas2005approximation,quarteroni2014reduced}.  For nonlinear,
transport-dominated systems, an accurate linear subspace may nevertheless
require many coordinates.  Nonlinear trial manifolds, learned closures, and
latent representations seek to reduce the number of online unknowns without
discarding the projection principle
\cite{barnett2023neural,cohen2023nonlinear,aghili2026nonlinear}.

This work compares intrusive closure-enhanced PROMs (PROM--ANN), an intrusive
decoder manifold (PROM--POD--AE), a non-intrusive direct coefficient map
(POD--NN--ROM), and a non-intrusive latent map (POD--DL--ROM).  PROM means
that the full time-discrete residual is minimized online.  It is not
synonymous with a generic ROM: the non-intrusive baselines do not evaluate a
residual online.  Likewise, the HPROM formulation below is a sampled-residual
extension of PROM, not a second name for the same computation.

The reported campaign uses a standard Euclidean POD basis and a common
full-residual linear PROM teacher.  This permits a direct comparison between
Case~2, which prescribes a learned secondary-coordinate tail but solves a
primary coordinate block, and POD--NN--ROM, which predicts the full coordinate
vector from the same parameter--time map.  Hyperreduction is developed
explicitly because an ECM rule must be tied to the trial manifold and its LSPG
tangent; HPROM numerical results are deliberately deferred until the
corresponding campaign is available.

\section{Projection Framework}
\label{sec:projection-framework}
\subsection{Time-discrete residual and Euclidean POD basis}
Consider a parametric nonlinear semi-discrete initial-value problem
\begin{equation}
\mathbf M(\mub)\dot{\uvec}(t;\mub)+\mathbf f(\uvec(t;\mub);\mub)
-\mathbf g(t;\mub)=\mathbf0,\qquad \uvec(0;\mub)=\uvec_0(\mub),
\label{eq:ivp}
\end{equation}
with $\mub\in\mathcal D$.  After a chosen implicit time discretization, let
$\mathbf r^{m+1}(\uvec;\mub)\in\mathbb R^N$ denote the residual at step
$m+1$, with dependence on the previous state understood.  The HDM solves
\begin{equation}
\mathbf r^{m+1}(\uvec^{m+1};\mub)=\mathbf0.
\label{eq:discrete-residual}
\end{equation}
Let $\uvec_{\mathrm{ref}}$ be the snapshot mean and let $\mathbf S_c$ collect
the centered HDM snapshots.  The thin singular-value decomposition
\begin{equation}
\mathbf S_c=\mathbf U\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf Y^T
\end{equation}
defines the Euclidean POD basis retained in this work,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf V_{\mathrm{tot}}=\mathbf U(:,1:n_{\mathrm{tot}}),\qquad
\mathbf V_{\mathrm{tot}}^T\mathbf V_{\mathrm{tot}}=\mathbf I.
\label{eq:euclidean-pod}
\end{equation}
The common affine linear trial manifold is
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\phi_N(\q_N)=\uvec_{\mathrm{ref}}+
\mathbf V_{\mathrm{tot}}\q_N,\qquad
\q_N\in\mathbb R^{n_{\mathrm{tot}}}.
\label{eq:linear-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
All learned trial maps below are expressed in these same coordinates.

\subsection{Linear PROM and LSPG reference}
The full-residual linear PROM is the time-discrete least-squares
Petrov--Galerkin (LSPG) problem
\begin{equation}
\q_N^{m+1}(\mub)=\arg\min_{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^{n_{\mathrm{tot}}}}
\norm{\mathbf r^{m+1}(\boldsymbol\phi_N(\mathbf x);\mub)}_2^2.
\label{eq:linear-prom}
\end{equation}
At Gauss--Newton iterate $k$, define
\begin{equation}
\mathbf T_N=\mathbf V_{\mathrm{tot}},\qquad
\mathbf J^{m+1,k}=
\left.\frac{\partial\mathbf r^{m+1}}{\partial\uvec}\right|_{
\boldsymbol\phi_N(\q_N^{m+1,k})},\qquad
\mathbf W_N^{m+1,k}=\mathbf J^{m+1,k}\mathbf T_N.
\end{equation}
The normal equations for the increment are
\begin{equation}
\left(\mathbf W_N^{m+1,k}\right)^T\mathbf W_N^{m+1,k}
\Delta\q_N^{m+1,k}=-
\left(\mathbf W_N^{m+1,k}\right)^T
\mathbf r^{m+1}\!\left(\boldsymbol\phi_N(\q_N^{m+1,k});\mub\right).
\label{eq:linear-prom-gn}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:linear-prom} generates all training targets in the PROM
campaign.

\subsection{Linear HPROM and ECM formulation}
\label{sec:linear-hprom}
The linear HPROM replaces full residual evaluations in the LSPG update by a
sparse, positive empirical-cubature approximation.  This formulation is
given in detail because the same projected-residual algebra is used when the
affine trial manifold is replaced by a learned one.  Let
$\mathcal E=\{e_i\}_{i=1}^{N_e}$ be the mesh entities.  The time-discrete
residual is assembled from local contributions as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf r^{m+1}(\uvec;\mub)=\sum_{e_i\in\mathcal E}\mathbf L_{e_i}^T
\mathbf r_{e_i}^{m+1}(\mathbf L_{e_i^+}\uvec;\mub).
\label{eq:entity-residual}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf r_{e_i}^{m+1}\in\mathbb R^{d_{e_i}}$ is the contribution of
entity $e_i$, $\mathbf L_{e_i}\in\{0,1\}^{d_{e_i}\times N}$ extracts its
residual degrees of freedom, and $\mathbf L_{e_i^+}$ extracts the enlarged
state stencil needed to evaluate it.  The stencil contains neighbouring
entities whenever the discretization requires them.

For the affine trial map \eqref{eq:linear-reconstruction}, the projected
LSPG residual at a Gauss--Newton iterate has the entity-wise form
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf g_N^{m+1,k}(\mub)
&:=\left(\mathbf W_N^{m+1,k}\right)^T
\mathbf r^{m+1}\!\left(\boldsymbol\phi_N(\q_N^{m+1,k});\mub\right)\\
&=\sum_{e_i\in\mathcal E}
\left(\mathbf L_{e_i}\mathbf W_N^{m+1,k}\right)^T
\mathbf r_{e_i}^{m+1}\!\left(
\mathbf L_{e_i^+}\boldsymbol\phi_N(\q_N^{m+1,k});\mub\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:linear-projected-residual}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbf J_{e_i}^{m+1,k}$ be the derivative of the local residual with
respect to the stencil state at the same iterate.  The Gauss--Newton matrix
is assembled analogously,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf A_N^{m+1,k}:=
\left(\mathbf W_N^{m+1,k}\right)^T\mathbf J^{m+1,k}\mathbf T_N
=\sum_{e_i\in\mathcal E}
\left(\mathbf L_{e_i}\mathbf W_N^{m+1,k}\right)^T
\mathbf J_{e_i}^{m+1,k}\left(\mathbf L_{e_i^+}\mathbf T_N\right).
\label{eq:linear-projected-jacobian}
\end{equation}

ECM selects a reduced entity set $\widetilde{\mathcal E}_N\subset\mathcal E$
and positive weights $\xi_{N,e_i}$.  The same rule approximates both
projected quantities,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\mathbf g}_N^{m+1,k}
&=\sum_{e_i\in\widetilde{\mathcal E}_N}\xi_{N,e_i}
\left(\mathbf L_{e_i}\mathbf W_N^{m+1,k}\right)^T
\mathbf r_{e_i}^{m+1}\!\left(
\mathbf L_{e_i^+}\boldsymbol\phi_N(\q_N^{m+1,k});\mub\right),\\
\widehat{\mathbf A}_N^{m+1,k}
&=\sum_{e_i\in\widetilde{\mathcal E}_N}\xi_{N,e_i}
\left(\mathbf L_{e_i}\mathbf W_N^{m+1,k}\right)^T
\mathbf J_{e_i}^{m+1,k}\left(\mathbf L_{e_i^+}\mathbf T_N\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:linear-ecm-sampled-normal-equations}
\end{equation}
The online sampled update is consequently
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathbf A}_N^{m+1,k}\Delta\q_N^{m+1,k}
=-\widehat{\mathbf g}_N^{m+1,k},\qquad
\q_N^{m+1,k+1}=\q_N^{m+1,k}+\Delta\q_N^{m+1,k}.
\label{eq:linear-hprom-gn-update}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Offline cubature construction.}
For every selected parameter--time training state $s$, the linear coordinate
$\q_{N,s}$ and its LSPG test basis $\mathbf W_{N,s}$ define the contribution
of entity $e_i$ to the projected residual,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf c_{N,e_i}^{(s)}=(\mathbf L_{e_i}\mathbf W_{N,s})^T
\mathbf r_{e_i}(\mathbf L_{e_i^+}\boldsymbol\phi_N(\q_{N,s});\mub_s).
\label{eq:linear-ecm-contribution}
\end{equation}
Stacking all $n_{\mathrm{tot}}$-component contributions over
$N_{\mathrm{ECM}}$ selected states gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbf C_N=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf c_{N,e_1}^{(1)} & \cdots & \mathbf c_{N,e_{N_e}}^{(1)}\\
\vdots & & \vdots\\
\mathbf c_{N,e_1}^{(N_{\mathrm{ECM}})} & \cdots &
\mathbf c_{N,e_{N_e}}^{(N_{\mathrm{ECM}})}
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb R^{n_{\mathrm{tot}}N_{\mathrm{ECM}}\times N_e},
\qquad \mathbf b_N=\mathbf C_N\mathbf1.
\label{eq:linear-ecm-training-matrix}
\end{equation}
ECM seeks a sparse nonnegative approximation of the full projected-residual
data,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\xi_N=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol\zeta\geq\mathbf0}
\norm{\mathbf C_N\boldsymbol\zeta-\mathbf b_N}_2^2,\qquad
\widetilde{\mathcal E}_N=\{e_i\in\mathcal E:\xi_{N,e_i}>0\}.
\label{eq:linear-ecm}
\end{equation}
In the present implementation, a direct SVD removes numerically null
directions of $\mathbf C_N$ before the nonnegative cubature solve.  The rule
is therefore tied to the affine trial manifold and its LSPG test basis.  In
particular, replacing $\boldsymbol\phi_N$ by a learned closure or decoder
also changes the tangent, the test basis, and the projected contributions;
the corresponding ECM rule must be constructed for that map.

\paragraph{Relation to other hyperreduction methods.}
The common step is to approximate an entity-wise sum of \emph{projected}
residual contributions, rather than the state reconstruction or a learned
map.  We use ECM in the sense of empirical cubature
\cite{hernandez2017dimensional,hernandez2020multiscale}.  ECSW is another
project--then--approximate hyperreduction strategy
\cite{farhat2015structure}.  It could be used with the same LSPG and trial
manifold formulations, but ECM is the rule used in this work.

\section{Learned Reduced Representations}
\label{sec:learned-representations}
\subsection{PROM--ANN closures}
Partition the common POD basis and coordinate vector as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf V_{\mathrm{tot}}=[\mathbf V\ \overline{\mathbf V}],\qquad
\q_N=\begin{bmatrix}\qp\\\qs\end{bmatrix},\qquad
n+\bar n=n_{\mathrm{tot}}.
\label{eq:partition}
\end{equation}
PROM--ANN supplies the secondary block through a learned relation
\begin{equation}
\qs=\mathcal F(\qp,\mub,t),\qquad
\boldsymbol\phi_{\mathcal F}(\qp;\mub,t)=\uvec_{\mathrm{ref}}+
\mathbf V\qp+\overline{\mathbf V}\mathcal F(\qp,\mub,t).
\label{eq:closure-manifold}
\end{equation}
The network provides reduced secondary coordinates, not a direct HDM state
approximation.  The primary coordinates remain online unknowns of a residual
minimization.  This is the neural-network-augmented projection construction
of \cite{barnett2023neural}; analogous closure roles have also been
represented by non-neural regressors \cite{ares2026closure}.

The three cases are information patterns in this common construction:
\begin{align}
\text{Case 1:}\quad&\qs=\mathcal N(\qp),&
\text{Case 2:}\quad&\qs=\mathcal M(\mub,t),&
\text{Case 3:}\quad&\qs=\mathcal H(\qp,\mub,t).
\label{eq:closure-cases}
\end{align}
Case~1 is state-conditioned, Case~2 parameter--time-conditioned, and Case~3
combines both inputs.  The LSPG problem is
\begin{equation}
\qp^{m+1}(\mub)=\arg\min_{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n}
\norm{\mathbf r^{m+1}(\boldsymbol\phi_{\mathcal F}
(\mathbf x;\mub,t^{m+1});\mub)}_2^2,
\label{eq:closure-lspg}
\end{equation}
with tangent
\begin{equation}
\mathbf T_{\mathcal F}=\mathbf V+\overline{\mathbf V}
\frac{\partial\mathcal F}{\partial\qp},\qquad
\mathbf W_{\mathcal F}=\mathbf J\mathbf T_{\mathcal F}.
\label{eq:closure-tangent}
\end{equation}
For Case~2, $\partial\mathcal M/\partial\qp=\mathbf0$ and
$\mathbf T_{\mathcal M}=\mathbf V$.  Its tangent is therefore the cheapest,
but its prescribed secondary block is not corrected through a
state-dependent tangent.

\subsection{PROM--POD--AE and intrusive trial maps}
An autoencoder maps the complete linear coordinate vector through a latent
variable,
\begin{equation}
\z=\mathcal E(\q_N),\qquad
\widehat{\q}_N=\mathcal D(\z),\qquad \z\in\mathbb R^{n_z}.
\end{equation}
PROM--POD--AE uses the decoder as an intrusive nonlinear trial manifold,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\phi_{\mathrm{AE}}(\z)=\uvec_{\mathrm{ref}}+
\mathbf V_{\mathrm{tot}}\mathcal D(\z),\qquad
\mathbf T_{\mathrm{AE}}=\mathbf V_{\mathrm{tot}}
\frac{\partial\mathcal D}{\partial\z}.
\label{eq:podae-manifold}
\end{equation}
Its latent coordinate minimizes the same residual as
\eqref{eq:closure-lspg}, but over $\z$ rather than $\qp$.  This is distinct
from POD--DL--ROM, which is non-intrusive.

\subsection{Model-specific ECM construction}
\label{sec:nonlinear-ecm}
The HPROM extension does not reuse the linear rule in \eqref{eq:linear-ecm}.
For any intrusive map $\boldsymbol\phi_c$ and tangent $\mathbf T_c$, the
entity contributions use $\mathbf W_c=\mathbf J\mathbf T_c$.  A manifold
coordinate is first fitted at every selected HDM state,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf y_{c,s}=\arg\min_{\mathbf y}\norm{\uvec_s^{m_s+1}-
\boldsymbol\phi_c(\mathbf y;\mub_s,t^{m_s+1})}_2^2.
\label{eq:nonlinear-manifold-fit}
\end{equation}
For Case~2 this is linear least squares after subtracting the prescribed tail,
\begin{equation}
{\qp}_{2,s}=\mathbf V^\dagger\left[
\uvec_s^{m_s+1}-\uvec_{\mathrm{ref}}-
\overline{\mathbf V}\mathcal M(\mub_s,t^{m_s+1})\right].
\label{eq:case2-ecm-fit}
\end{equation}
For Cases~1 and~3 and for the decoder manifold, it is nonlinear least
squares, for example with the Gauss--Newton update
\begin{equation}
\mathbf y_{c,s}^{(k+1)}=\mathbf y_{c,s}^{(k)}-
\left[\frac{\partial\boldsymbol\phi_c}{\partial\mathbf y}\right]^\dagger
\left[\boldsymbol\phi_c(\mathbf y_{c,s}^{(k)};\mub_s,t^{m_s+1})-
\uvec_s^{m_s+1}\right].
\label{eq:nonlinear-manifold-fit-gn}
\end{equation}
At the fitted state,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf c_{c,e_i}^{(s)}=(\mathbf L_{e_i}\mathbf W_{c,s})^T
\mathbf r_{e_i}(\mathbf L_{e_i^+}
\boldsymbol\phi_c(\mathbf y_{c,s};\mub_s,t^{m_s+1});\mub_s).
\label{eq:nonlinear-ecm-contribution}
\end{equation}
Stacking these vectors produces $\mathbf C_c$ and
$\mathbf b_c=\mathbf C_c\mathbf1$ as in \eqref{eq:linear-ecm}; a positive
ECM rule is then computed for each map.  The trained closure or decoder, its
tangent, and the parameter--time sample set are therefore part of a rule.  A
linear-HPROM rule, or one built for a different nonlinear map, cannot be
reused \cite{hernandez2017dimensional,hernandez2020multiscale,de2023hyper}.

\subsection{Non-intrusive reference models}
POD--NN--ROM is a direct map
\begin{equation}
\q_N(t;\mub)\approx\mathcal G_q(\mub,t),
\label{eq:podnn}
\end{equation}
and does not solve a residual online.  POD--DL--ROM uses a learned latent map
and decoder,
\begin{equation}
\z(t;\mub)\approx\mathcal G_z(\mub,t),\qquad
\q_N(t;\mub)\approx\mathcal D(\mathcal G_z(\mub,t)),
\label{eq:poddl}
\end{equation}
following the POD--DL--ROM principle \cite{fresca2022pod}.  These are direct
reduced-coordinate regressors, not PROM residual solves.

\section{Benchmark and PROM Protocol}
\label{sec:benchmark}
\subsection{Burgers problem and discretization}
We use the two-dimensional parametric inviscid Burgers benchmark of
\cite{barnett2023neural},
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathbf U}{\partial t}+
\frac{\partial\mathbf F(\mathbf U)}{\partial x}+
\frac{\partial\mathbf G(\mathbf U)}{\partial y}=\mathbf S(x;\mub),\qquad
\mathbf U=\begin{bmatrix}u_x\\u_y\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\mathbf F(\mathbf U)=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac12u_x^2\\\tfrac12u_xu_y\end{bmatrix},
\quad\mathbf G(\mathbf U)=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac12u_xu_y\\\tfrac12u_y^2\end{bmatrix},
\quad\mathbf S(x;\mub)=\begin{bmatrix}0.02\exp(\mu_2x)\\0\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The domain is $[0,100]^2$, $T_f=25$, and
$\mathcal D=[4.25,5.50]\times[0.015,0.03]$.  The HDM uses a cell-centered
finite-volume Godunov discretization on a $250\times250$ grid
($N=125{,}000$) and $500$ uniform time steps with $\Delta t=0.05$.

\subsection{Data, validation, and online points}
Nine HDM trajectories on a $3\times3$ tensor grid define the centered
snapshot matrix for the Euclidean POD.  We retain $n_{\mathrm{tot}}=151$
modes, which capture $99.9900\%$ of its energy.  At these same nine parameters
we solve the full-residual linear PROM.  The resulting PROM-generated
trajectories, rather than HDM snapshots orthogonally projected after the fact,
are the learning targets for every model.  This comparison-control choice
aligns every learned model with the reduced optimality problem used as its
teacher; it is not the conventional purely non-intrusive workflow, which
typically uses projected HDM labels.

Two complete 151-coordinate linear-PROM trajectories are reserved before
training,
\begin{equation}
\mub^{\mathrm{val},1}=(4.5625,0.02625),\qquad
\mub^{\mathrm{val},2}=(5.1875,0.01875).
\label{eq:validation-points}
\end{equation}
Because each held-out parameter contains all time samples, this is a
parameter-generalization test rather than a random time-snapshot split.  The
four online evaluations are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mub^{(v)}&=(4.875,0.0225),&
\mub^{(1)}&=(4.560,0.0190),\\
\mub^{(2)}&=(5.190,0.0260),&
\mub^{(3)}&=(4.000,0.0330).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:evaluation-points}
\end{equation}
The first three are in-domain and $\mub^{(3)}$ is extrapolatory.  The
validation locations are neither training nor online evaluation points.

\subsection{Nested coefficient-data enrichment}
To isolate data coverage from architecture and online formulation, we use a
fixed seed--42 Latin-hypercube candidate design over the original parameter
box and a $25\%$ coordinate-wise margin.  Two deterministic nested subsets are
retained: $9+8$ contains the baseline grid, four interior points, and four
margin points; $9+18$ contains the same baseline and early points plus five
additional interior and five additional margin points.  The complete
$9+36$ candidate design defines the nested ordering only; no $9+36$ PROM
coefficient campaign is used in this study.  All online evaluation and
parameter-validation locations are excluded from the enrichment sets.

The word ``margin'' denotes the union of the left, right, top, and bottom
strips of the expanded rectangle.  It does not enforce coverage of their
intersections.  This distinction matters for the upper-left extrapolation
point $\mub^{(3)}$ and will be revisited with the online results.

For the controlled Case--2 comparison, one master map is trained as

\end{equation}
For primary dimension $n$, Case~2 inserts
$\qs=\mathbf P_{>n}\mathcal G_q(\mub,t)$ into the residual solve, whereas
POD--NN--ROM uses the entire vector directly.  Thus $n=0$ is exactly the
POD--NN--ROM endpoint from this same trained map, and $n=151$ is the linear
PROM endpoint.  The shared-map construction is a diagnostic control, not a
restriction of the general Case--2 formulation.

\subsection{Reported errors}
For each evaluation parameter, the state error is
\begin{equation}
e_u(\mub)=100\frac{\norm{\uvec_{\mathrm{HDM}}-\uvec_{\mathrm{ROM}}}_F}
{\norm{\uvec_{\mathrm{HDM}}}_F},
\label{eq:state-error}
\end{equation}
and the coefficient error against the full linear PROM trajectory is
\begin{equation}
e_q(\mub)=100\frac{\norm{\q_{151}^{\mathrm{lin}}-\widehat{\q}_{151}}_F}
{\norm{\q_{151}^{\mathrm{lin}}}_F}.
\label{eq:coefficient-error}
\end{equation}
The linear PROM is an accuracy reference for the fixed basis and full
residual, not a universal lower bound with respect to the HDM.

\subsection{Controlled Case--2 tail perturbation}
\label{sec:tail-perturbation}
To test the transfer mechanism independently of a regression magnitude, we
start from the linear-PROM secondary history $\qs^{\mathrm{lin}}$ and add a
perturbation in the direction of the actual master-map secondary error:
\begin{equation}
{\qs}_{\epsilon}=\qs^{\mathrm{lin}}+
\frac{\epsilon}{100}\frac{\widehat{\qs}-\qs^{\mathrm{lin}}}
{\norm{\widehat{\qs}-\qs^{\mathrm{lin}}}_F}
\norm{\qs^{\mathrm{lin}}}_F.
\label{eq:tail-perturbation}
\end{equation}
For each prescribed percentage $\epsilon$, Case~2 solves only the
first ten coordinates.  Thus the diagnostic uses the observed ANN-error
direction but does not conflate its effect with a change of learned model.
Table~\ref{tab:tail-perturbation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:tail-perturbation} show a
monotone transfer from secondary-tail error to the recovered primary block.
The amount of transfer is parameter dependent, so a tail loss alone does not
imply a pointwise primary-coordinate bound.

\section{Effect of PROM Coefficient-Data Enrichment}
\label{sec:euclidean-prom-enrichment}
The nested campaign tests whether the baseline discrepancies are primarily
caused by the learned architecture or by parameter-space coverage.  The
Euclidean basis, network architectures, two parameter-validation trajectories,
four online evaluation points, and full-residual PROM formulations are fixed.
Only the number and location of linear-PROM coefficient-training trajectories
change through the nested budgets $9$, $9+8$, and $9+18$.

\subsection{Offline regression accuracy}
Table~\ref{tab:euclidean-prom-enrichment-training} reports errors in the
reduced coordinates used to train and select the networks.  All five learned
families improve on the same external parameter-validation set as data are
added.  The validation error of the master parameter--time map shared by
Case~2 and POD--NN--ROM decreases from $4.261\%$ to $1.607\%$ with $9+8$ and
to $0.990\%$ with $9+18$.  This monotone held-out improvement does not,
however, imply monotone accuracy at a more distant extrapolation point.
The three Euclidean master maps use the same wide SiLU architecture, raw MSE
objective, external validation data, and random seed.  Their selected
checkpoints occur at epochs 1010, 1634, and 1731 for the baseline, $9+8$, and
$9+18$ data, respectively, under the same validation-based early-stopping
rule; none is the result of a wall-time interruption.

coefficient-data coverage is the dominant limitation in domain: already
$9+8$ substantially improves every model, and $9+18$ brings all reported

itself does not control extrapolation.  A small nested subset can reduce
held-out in-domain validation error while worsening a corner extrapolation if
its geometry does not cover the relevant joint parameter direction.

\section{Conclusions}
The Euclidean-POD PROM campaign gives a common full-residual comparison of
three learned closures, an intrusive decoder, and two direct
reduced-coordinate maps.  The linear PROM remains the most accurate model in
the fixed 151-coordinate trial representation.  Case~3 and PROM--POD--AE
retain close in-domain state accuracy while solving a low-dimensional online
variable.  The Case--2/POD--NN shared-map study gives a precise interpretation
of intrusive correction: solving progressively more coordinates through the
residual moves the result from the direct-map endpoint toward linear PROM, but
the prescribed tail can still transfer its error into the solved block.
The nested enrichment study shows that $9+18$ trajectories are sufficient to
bring all learned models close to the linear-PROM reference at the reported
points.  The $9+8$ campaign improves every in-domain result but exposes a
strong, localized master-map extrapolation failure, demonstrating that the
geometry of added samples matters in addition to their number.

The results intentionally do not mix PROM evidence with an unavailable HPROM
data set.  The ECM derivation establishes the required model-specific
construction: an HPROM rule must be built from each learned trial map and its
tangent, rather than borrowed from a linear HPROM or from a different closure.
The next HPROM campaign can therefore use the same reporting order, tables,
and model comparisons as the PROM study.

\appendix
\section{LSPG-Sensitive Basis Exploration}
\label{app:lspg-sensitive}
Before adopting the Euclidean POD basis used in the main text, we explored an
$M$-regularized basis metric intended to emphasize sensitivity of the LSPG
normal equations to reduced-coordinate perturbations.  The objective was to
reduce transfer from a learned secondary block to residual-solved primary
coordinates without degrading the linear reference.  In the present Burgers
campaign, controlled tests did not establish a systematic improvement
sufficient to justify making that metric the main experimental variable.  For
clarity and reproducibility, every reported result therefore uses the standard
Euclidean POD basis of \eqref{eq:euclidean-pod}.  The metric-aware
construction is retained as an exploratory direction, not as a contribution
or as a source of the numerical comparisons in this paper.  The historical
LSPG-sensitive enrichment campaign also selected an ELU master map, whereas
the Euclidean campaign uses SiLU.  Its earlier extrapolation trend is therefore
not an architecture-controlled metric comparison and cannot be attributed to
the basis choice alone.
