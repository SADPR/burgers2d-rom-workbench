\section{Purpose of this note}
This note rewrites Prof. Maday's proposal using the same symbols as your manuscript:
\[
\mathbf V_{\mathrm{tot}}=[\mathbf V\ \overline{\mathbf V}],
\qquad
\mathbf q_N=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf q\\
\bar{\mathbf q}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\tilde{\mathbf u}=\mathbf u_{\mathrm{ref}}+\mathbf V\mathbf q+\overline{\mathbf V}\bar{\mathbf q}.
\]
The goal is to explain, step by step, why small errors in high coefficients can strongly damage low-coefficient recovery in LSPG, and what three concrete remedies are being proposed.

\section{Where the issue comes from (in your current formulation)}
In Case 2 of your manuscript, the closure gives
\[
\bar{\mathbf q}=\mathcal M(\bm\mu,t),
\]
and the online unknown is only $\mathbf q$, obtained by solving an LSPG least-squares problem at each time step.

In practice, this means:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\bar{\mathbf q}$ is injected from a regressor (ANN, GPR, etc.),
\item then LSPG solves for $\mathbf q$.
\end{itemize}

Prof. Maday's key observation is:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item if $\bar{\mathbf q}$ is exact, LSPG can recover a good $\mathbf q$,
\item but if $\bar{\mathbf q}$ has even a small error, the solved $\mathbf q$ can drift a lot.
\end{itemize}

So the true problem is not only ``predict high coefficients accurately''; it is also ``make the reduced basis split robust so that high-mode errors do not contaminate low modes too much''.

\section{Linearized contamination mechanism: full derivation}
Let $\mathbf r(\mathbf u;\bm\mu)$ be the time-discrete residual and $\mathbf J=\partial \mathbf r/\partial \mathbf u$ the Jacobian at the current linearization point.

Assume the residual norm is weighted by a symmetric positive matrix $\mathbf P$:
\[
\|\mathbf r\|_{\mathbf P}^2=\mathbf r^T\mathbf P\,\mathbf r.
\]
(If Euclidean residual norm is used, $\mathbf P=\mathbf I$.)

Define the low/high Jacobian blocks
\[
\mathbf J_L:=\mathbf J\mathbf V,
\qquad
\mathbf J_H:=\mathbf J\overline{\mathbf V}.
\]

\subsection*{Step 1: local LSPG objective in low coordinates}
For fixed time step and parameter, define
\[
\Phi(\mathbf q;\bar{\mathbf q})
:=
\frac12\left\|
\mathbf r\!\left(
\mathbf u_{\mathrm{ref}}+\mathbf V\mathbf q+\overline{\mathbf V}\bar{\mathbf q}
;\bm\mu\right)
\right\|_{\mathbf P}^2.
\]
Here $\Phi$ is the local least-squares objective seen by the low coordinates $\mathbf q$ when the high coordinates $\bar{\mathbf q}$ are injected/fixed by closure.
At a local optimum $(\mathbf q,\bar{\mathbf q})$, first-order optimality with respect to $\mathbf q$ gives
\[
\nabla_{\mathbf q}\Phi
=
\mathbf J_L^T\mathbf P\,\mathbf r
=
\mathbf0.
\]

\subsection*{Step 2: perturb only the high block}
Now perturb the high block:
\[
\bar{\mathbf q}\mapsto \bar{\mathbf q}+\delta\bar{\mathbf q},
\]
and let the low solution shift to $\mathbf q+\delta\mathbf q$.
Linearizing the residual:
\[
\mathbf r(\mathbf q+\delta\mathbf q,\bar{\mathbf q}+\delta\bar{\mathbf q})
\approx
\mathbf r
+\mathbf J_L\,\delta\mathbf q
+\mathbf J_H\,\delta\bar{\mathbf q}.
\]

\subsection*{Step 3: linearized least-squares normal equations}
With fixed $\delta\bar{\mathbf q}$, the local problem in $\delta\mathbf q$ is
\[
\min_{\delta\mathbf q}
\frac12
\left\|
\mathbf r+\mathbf J_L\delta\mathbf q+\mathbf J_H\delta\bar{\mathbf q}
\right\|_{\mathbf P}^2.
\]
Normal equations:
\[
\mathbf J_L^T\mathbf P
\left(
\mathbf r+\mathbf J_L\delta\mathbf q+\mathbf J_H\delta\bar{\mathbf q}
\right)
=
\mathbf0.
\]
Subtracting the unperturbed stationarity condition $\mathbf J_L^T\mathbf P\mathbf r=\mathbf0$:
\[
\mathbf J_L^T\mathbf P\mathbf J_L\,\delta\mathbf q
+
\mathbf J_L^T\mathbf P\mathbf J_H\,\delta\bar{\mathbf q}
=
\mathbf0.
\]
If $\mathbf J_L^T\mathbf P\mathbf J_L$ is invertible, then
\[
\delta\mathbf q
=
-\left(\mathbf J_L^T\mathbf P\mathbf J_L\right)^{-1}
\mathbf J_L^T\mathbf P\mathbf J_H\,\delta\bar{\mathbf q}.
\]

\subsection*{Step 4: transfer operator}
Define
\[
\mathbf T
:=
\left(\mathbf J_L^T\mathbf P\mathbf J_L\right)^{-1}
\mathbf J_L^T\mathbf P\mathbf J_H.
\]
Then
\[
\delta\mathbf q=-\mathbf T\,\delta\bar{\mathbf q}.
\]
So if $\|\mathbf T\|$ is large, small high-block errors can generate large low-block errors.

\paragraph{Plain-language interpretation.}
This is exactly your symptom: even when the high map looks ``reasonable'' on some metric, the online LSPG solve can still become fragile because the low/high decomposition is not well decoupled in the \emph{LSPG sensitivity metric}.

\section{Proposal 1: use a better POD metric}
\subsection{Weighted POD baseline}
Instead of Euclidean SVD, use weighted POD with SPD matrix $\mathbf W$:
\[
\mathbf C=\mathbf S^T\mathbf W\mathbf S.
\]
Here $\mathbf S=[\mathbf s_1,\dots,\mathbf s_{N_s}]\in\mathbb R^{N\times N_s}$ is the snapshot matrix (usually centered snapshots), and $\mathbf W\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}$ defines the state-space inner product.
\paragraph{Where this comes from (derivation).}
For one POD mode $\bm\phi$, weighted POD maximizes captured weighted snapshot energy:
\[
\max_{\bm\phi\neq\mathbf0}
\frac{\bm\phi^T\mathbf W\mathbf S\mathbf S^T\mathbf W\bm\phi}
{\bm\phi^T\mathbf W\bm\phi}.
\]
The associated stationarity condition is
\[
\mathbf S\mathbf S^T\mathbf W\bm\phi=\lambda\bm\phi.
\]
Using method of snapshots, write $\bm\phi=\mathbf S\mathbf z$ (mode in the snapshot span). Substituting:
\[
\mathbf S^T\mathbf W\mathbf S\,\mathbf z=\lambda\mathbf z
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbf C\mathbf z=\lambda\mathbf z.
\]
Then the weighted POD mode is reconstructed by
\[
\bm\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\mathbf S\mathbf z,
\]
with normalization $\bm\phi^T\mathbf W\bm\phi=1$.

For your two-component Burgers state, Prof. Maday suggests the natural state metric
\[
\mathbf W=\mathbf M_{\mathrm{block}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf M_h & \mathbf 0\\
\mathbf 0 & \mathbf M_h
\end{bmatrix},
\]
which corresponds to a discrete $L^2$-type norm of $(u_x,u_y)$ instead of a plain coordinate-vector norm.

\subsection{LSPG-aware enriched metric}
To directly encode LSPG sensitivity into basis extraction, use
\[
\mathbf W_{\mathrm{eff}}
=
\varepsilon\,\mathbf M_{\mathrm{block}}
\;+\;
\sum_s \omega_s\,\mathbf J_s^T\mathbf P_s\mathbf J_s,
\qquad
\omega_s\ge 0,\ \sum_s\omega_s=1.
\]
Here each sample $s$ corresponds to a representative time-parameter point.
\paragraph{Where this comes from (derivation).}
Around a sample $s$, for a small state perturbation $\delta\mathbf u$, residual linearization gives
\[
\delta\mathbf r_s\approx \mathbf J_s\,\delta\mathbf u.
\]
Hence the weighted residual change is
\[
\|\delta\mathbf r_s\|_{\mathbf P_s}^2
\approx
\delta\mathbf u^T\mathbf J_s^T\mathbf P_s\mathbf J_s\,\delta\mathbf u.
\]
So $\mathbf J_s^T\mathbf P_s\mathbf J_s$ is the local quadratic form measuring ``LSPG sensitivity'' to perturbations.
A weighted average over representative samples yields
\[
\mathbf A:=\sum_s\omega_s\,\mathbf J_s^T\mathbf P_s\mathbf J_s.
\]
Adding $\varepsilon\mathbf M_{\mathrm{block}}$ keeps a baseline state metric and regularization:
\[
\mathbf W_{\mathrm{eff}}=\varepsilon\mathbf M_{\mathrm{block}}+\mathbf A.
\]

\paragraph{Plain-language interpretation.}
The second term penalizes directions that strongly affect residual minimization, so the basis is adapted to what LSPG actually ``cares about'', not only to snapshot energy.

\section{Proposal 2: modify only high modes (keep $\mathbf V$ fixed)}
This is the proposal Prof. Maday emphasized as attractive because online dimension and online solve structure stay unchanged.

Define averaged LSPG metric
\[
\mathbf A:=\sum_s \omega_s\,\mathbf J_s^T\mathbf P_s\mathbf J_s.
\]
Then define blocks
\[
\mathbf H_{LL}:=\mathbf V^T\mathbf A\mathbf V,
\qquad
\mathbf H_{LH}:=\mathbf V^T\mathbf A\overline{\mathbf V}.
\]
\paragraph{Derivation of the correction formula.}
Seek a corrected high basis in the form
\[
\widetilde{\overline{\mathbf V}}=\overline{\mathbf V}-\mathbf V\mathbf K,
\]
where $\mathbf K$ is unknown.
Impose metric-orthogonality against low modes:
\[
\mathbf V^T\mathbf A\widetilde{\overline{\mathbf V}}=\mathbf0.
\]
Substitute:
\[
\mathbf V^T\mathbf A(\overline{\mathbf V}-\mathbf V\mathbf K)
=
\mathbf H_{LH}-\mathbf H_{LL}\mathbf K
=
\mathbf0.
\]
So
\[
\mathbf H_{LL}\mathbf K=\mathbf H_{LH}.
\]
If $\mathbf H_{LL}$ is invertible,
\[
\mathbf K=\mathbf H_{LL}^{-1}\mathbf H_{LH}.
\]
Hence
\[
\widetilde{\overline{\mathbf V}}
=
\overline{\mathbf V}
-\mathbf V\,\mathbf H_{LL}^{-1}\mathbf H_{LH}.
\]
\paragraph{Direct verification.}
\[
\mathbf V^T\mathbf A\widetilde{\overline{\mathbf V}}
=
\mathbf H_{LH}-\mathbf H_{LL}\mathbf H_{LL}^{-1}\mathbf H_{LH}
=
\mathbf0.
\]

\paragraph{What this buys you.}
Low modes are untouched, high modes are ``orthogonalized'' against low modes in the averaged LSPG metric, and the low/high contamination channel is reduced.

\paragraph{Coordinate mapping note (important).}
Let
\[
\mathbf K:=\mathbf H_{LL}^{-1}\mathbf H_{LH}.
\]
Because $\widetilde{\overline{\mathbf V}}=\overline{\mathbf V}-\mathbf V\mathbf K$, the same state can be written as
\[
\mathbf u_{\mathrm{ref}}+\mathbf V\mathbf q+\overline{\mathbf V}\bar{\mathbf q}
=
\mathbf u_{\mathrm{ref}}+\mathbf V\mathbf q+\left(\widetilde{\overline{\mathbf V}}+\mathbf V\mathbf K\right)\bar{\mathbf q}
=
\mathbf u_{\mathrm{ref}}+\mathbf V\left(\mathbf q+\mathbf K\bar{\mathbf q}\right)+\widetilde{\overline{\mathbf V}}\,\bar{\mathbf q}.
\]
with one convenient choice
\[
\widetilde{\bar{\mathbf q}}=\bar{\mathbf q},
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathbf q}=\mathbf q+\mathbf K\bar{\mathbf q}.
\]
So the ``high'' coordinates can be kept as-is, while low coordinates are shifted consistently.

\section{Proposal 3: modify only low modes (keep $\overline{\mathbf V}$ fixed)}
Define
\[
\mathbf H_{HH}:=\overline{\mathbf V}^T\mathbf A\overline{\mathbf V},
\qquad
\mathbf H_{HL}:=\overline{\mathbf V}^T\mathbf A\mathbf V.
\]
\paragraph{Derivation of the correction formula.}
Keep $\overline{\mathbf V}$ fixed and seek
\[
\widetilde{\mathbf V}=\mathbf V-\overline{\mathbf V}\mathbf L
\]
such that
\[
\overline{\mathbf V}^T\mathbf A\widetilde{\mathbf V}=\mathbf0.
\]
Substitute:
\[
\overline{\mathbf V}^T\mathbf A(\mathbf V-\overline{\mathbf V}\mathbf L)
=
\mathbf H_{HL}-\mathbf H_{HH}\mathbf L
=
\mathbf0.
\]
Thus
\[
\mathbf H_{HH}\mathbf L=\mathbf H_{HL},
\qquad
\mathbf L=\mathbf H_{HH}^{-1}\mathbf H_{HL},
\]
and therefore
\[
\widetilde{\mathbf V}
=
\mathbf V
-\overline{\mathbf V}\,\mathbf H_{HH}^{-1}\mathbf H_{HL}.
\]
\paragraph{Direct verification.}
\[
\overline{\mathbf V}^T\mathbf A\widetilde{\mathbf V}
=
\mathbf H_{HL}-\mathbf H_{HH}\mathbf H_{HH}^{-1}\mathbf H_{HL}
=
\mathbf0.
\]

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
This is the symmetric version: keep high block fixed, orthogonalize low block in the averaged LSPG metric.

\section{How this maps to your current pipeline}
\subsection*{Step A: build $\mathbf A$ on representative samples}
Use a subset of Stage-2 trajectories (time and parameter points), compute Jacobians $\mathbf J_s$, and assemble
\[
\mathbf A\approx \sum_s \omega_s\,\mathbf J_s^T\mathbf P_s\mathbf J_s.
\]

\subsection*{Step B: choose one basis intervention}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Proposal 2 (high-only):} usually the least intrusive for Case 2, because low online unknown size remains $n$, and you can preserve interpretation of injected high block.
\item \textbf{Proposal 3 (low-only):} also valid, but often more invasive for current closure workflows.
\item \textbf{Proposal 1 (metric POD):} can be done independently or combined with Proposal 2/3.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Step C: regenerate consistent reduced data}
After basis modification, reduced coefficients should be made consistent with the new basis (either via exact coordinate transformation when available, or by recomputing projection/reduced trajectories).

\subsection*{Step D: retrain closure map and re-run offline/online tests}
Then compare:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item low/high coefficient errors,
\item LSPG residual norms,
\item robustness at off-grid $\bm\mu$ points.
\end{itemize}

\section{What Prof. Maday is really suggesting (one-sentence summary)}
Do not treat high-mode prediction error only as a regression problem; redesign the \emph{basis split and metric} so that LSPG dynamics is less sensitive to high-block perturbations.

\section{Practical recommendation for your immediate next test}
For your current Case-2 setup, the most direct first experiment is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item keep $n$ fixed and keep online algorithm unchanged,
\item compute $\mathbf A$ from a representative set,
\item apply Proposal 2 (high-mode correction),
\item run the same Stage-2/Stage-3/evaluation protocol and compare against your current baseline.
\end{enumerate}
This isolates whether reduced-basis decoupling improves robustness without changing the online model size.
